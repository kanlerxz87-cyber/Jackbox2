Trade area: what the numbers say, and at what geography

Geography discipline. A county figure is not a trade-area figure. Los Angeles County covers 4,059 square miles and 9.7 million people; a burger restaurant draws from roughly one to three miles. Using the county average to value this parcel would be a category error. Everything below is labelled with the geography it actually describes. The tract-level rows are blank until the parcel's APN is used to select the right tracts in the secure workflow, and this site will not guess a tract from a city name.

The single most important line for a landlord. Percentage rent fell $16.5 million in one year. Percentage rent is the landlord's share of the tenant's upside, and it only exists when a lease sets a sales threshold. If your lease contains a percentage-rent clause, its threshold is now harder to clear than when it was written, and any renewal negotiation should re-set that threshold rather than carry it forward.

The pattern is a four-year slide from strongly positive to persistently negative same-store sales, with transactions falling faster than the average check. That means fewer customers, not cheaper ones — the harder kind of decline for a landlord, because the tenant fixes it by closing units rather than by raising prices.

Two facts to sit with. First, 941 of 2,136 restaurants — 44% of the system — sit in the same state as your parcel, carrying the same wage law and largely the same seismic and insurance environment. Second, a shareholder who put $100 into the company in September 2020 held $28 five years later, against $214 for the S&P 500. Whatever the store-level economics are, the market has repriced this tenant's future sharply downward. That repricing is the single best argument for testing a sale while a buyer can still underwrite brand credit.

Note the tension: median household income of $90,112 is high in absolute terms, but the county has been losing population for five years and 13.3% of residents are below the poverty line. A high average with a shrinking base and a large low-income share is the mix that squeezes quick-service restaurants first, because customer counts fall before the average ticket does.

A note on two population series. The Census estimate (9,694,934 for July 2025) and the California Department of Finance estimate (a loss of 64,000 over the year to January 2026) are produced by different methods for different reference dates and are not interchangeable. Both are official. Neither is the trade area. They are listed on Flagged for Review as F-18 so the discrepancy is visible rather than averaged away.

7 · How to fill the blank rows without exposing the address

  1. Get the APN from your closing documents or the county recorder. Keep it offline.
  2. Obtain the tract for that APN using the Census geocoder in your own browser session. Do not paste the result into any public tool.
  3. Pull ACS 5-year tables for that tract and for the neighbouring tracts inside one-mile and three-mile rings. Record the table ID and the vintage for each figure, because tract boundaries change between vintages and mixing vintages invalidates the comparison.
  4. Record only the aggregate result here — a number and the ACS table link, never the address, never the APN.
  5. Keep the APN-to-tract mapping in the private workspace, not in this repository.
Why this changes the decision. Whether the trade area is growing or shrinking moves the renewal case more than any other local variable. A shrinking tract makes a marginal unit a closure candidate at the next franchisee review, which is precisely when a tenant declines to renew. Two adjacent tracts in Central Los Angeles can have opposite five-year trends, so the county average genuinely tells you nothing about this parcel.
