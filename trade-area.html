Trade area

County and metro context, verified and clearly labelled as context — plus the tract selection rule that replaces it, and the trade-area rows left blank until it is run.

W-01, stated precisely. City and county figures are proxies, and proxies are not evidence about a parcel. Replacing them requires the parcel's census tracts, and the parcel's location is deliberately not in this repository. So this page publishes the verified regional context, states clearly that it is context, and sets out the tract selection rule that will replace it. The table on this page will stay blank until that rule has been run inside the access-controlled workspace.

The tract selection rule

  1. Fix two rings, once. A three-mile radius and a ten-minute drive-time polygon measured in the midday off-peak window, which is the relevant window for a lunch-driven drive-through. Write both down with the date and the routing tool used.
  2. Include a tract when most of its population falls inside the ring. Use one rule and apply it to both rings. Record every included and excluded tract so the same list can be reused next year.
  3. Weight, do not average. Weight each tract by its share of the ring's population, so a large tract does not dominate a small dense one.
  4. Use five-year estimates, and two vintages for direction. One-year estimates are too noisy at tract level. Two matched five-year vintages give direction; a single vintage gives only a snapshot.
  5. Check comparability before comparing. Tract boundaries and table definitions change between vintages. If the geography changed, the comparison is invalid and must be labelled as such.
  6. Never mix a tract figure with a county figure in the same row. If a tract value is unavailable, the cell stays blank.

Field evidence the spreadsheet cannot replace

  • Traffic: a published count at the nearest station, plus a manual peak-hour count at the driveway.
  • Access and egress: whether a left turn into the site is permitted at peak, and whether the drive-through queue blocks the sidewalk.
  • Competitors: every limited-service restaurant inside the rings, with opening date and closure date where known.
  • Condition: roof, parking surface, signage, and whether the building would suit a second-generation restaurant use without demolition.
  • Development pipeline: planning applications within the rings, because new supply changes the renewal case more than a one-year sales figure does.
Why this matters to the decision. The renewal case needs the trade area to grow. The sale case needs a buyer to believe it will. Both rest on tract-level direction, which is currently untested. A county figure cannot carry that weight, and publishing one as if it could would be the single easiest way to make this workspace wrong.
