Sell before expiry, or renew?

A verified-evidence workspace for one anonymous single-tenant Jack in the Box net-lease parcel in Central Los Angeles. Every number links to the official page that printed it; anything unconfirmed is blank and flagged.

Bottom line. The evidence does not support choosing between selling before expiry and renewing. The tenant is still shrinking, the county population is falling, the rate environment is expensive, and the three inputs that decide the answer — the lease abstract, the parcel hazard and insurance file, and the owner's tax basis — are not in hand. What can be done now is to clear the gates in order, starting with the notice calendar, and to keep the decision reversible until the numbers exist. What the verified record shows about the tenant

Four filings tell a consistent story: the brand contracted through fiscal 2025, the decline slowed in the third quarter of fiscal 2026, and the estate is still getting smaller. Same-store sales fell 4.2% for FY2025 and 7.4% in the fourth quarter; 86 restaurants closed against 31 openings. By the third quarter of FY2026 same-store sales were down 1.1% instead of 7.1%, but the company still closed 17 restaurants against 4 openings and guided to a further low-single-digit decline for the year.

For a landlord, the more relevant line is rent. Franchise rental revenues fell from US$260.3 million to US$242.5 million over the first 40 weeks of FY2026, a 6.8% decline that the company links partly to its own closure programme. The tenant also refinanced US$500 million of secured debt at 7.624% with an anticipated repayment date of May 2031, suspended its dividend, and announced a chief financial officer transition effective April 2027. None of that is a statement about this parcel, but all of it bears on how much rent the operator can support and how a buyer will price the remaining term.
