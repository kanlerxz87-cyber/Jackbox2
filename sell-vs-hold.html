Sell vs hold

The after-tax module and the short-lease discount, defined precisely — with the verified tax rules and a refusal to publish a comparison built on missing inputs.

W-02, without a winner. A sell-versus-hold comparison needs two numbers computed on the same day from the same inputs: after-tax net sale proceeds, and the present value of holding. Only one of the inputs is available today — the statutory tax rules. The rent, the price, the basis and the exit cap rate are not. So this page publishes the method and the verified rules, and refuses to publish a comparison.

Hold Net present value = present value of net operating income over the remaining term + present value of the terminal sale at the exit cap rate, net of exit costs. Rent, escalation, owner-retained costs, vacancy allowance, discount rate, exit cap rate, remaining term The Treasury reference only. Rent, term and costs are private.

Because both branches are computed on the same date with the same discount rate, the difference between them is a single number the owner can act on: hold value minus after-tax sale proceeds. A positive difference favours holding on those inputs; a negative one favours selling. The calculator computes that difference and the price at which it becomes zero.

Short-lease discount — priced from quotes, not assumed

The discount for a short lease is not a single market number; it is the difference between what buyers pay for the same rent with a long term and with a short one. It can only be measured from comparable sales and broker opinions, using the cap rate each transaction implies:

Method. For the same rent, value is inversely proportional to the cap rate. If a comparable with a long lease trades at cap rate A and a comparable with a short lease trades at cap rate B, the observed short-lease discount is 1 − (A ÷ B), expressed as a percentage. The discount is then applied to the subject's long-lease value to establish what a buyer would pay today.

Example of the arithmetic only, with invented cap rates to show the shape: 5.50% against 6.75% implies 1 − (5.50 ÷ 6.75) = 18.5%. Those numbers are illustrative and are not a market observation. The calculator applies this formula to whatever cap rates the comparable set actually supports.

What would make this page a comparison

  1. A sale price from at least three written broker opinions.
  2. The adjusted basis and accumulated depreciation from the accountant.
  3. Rent, escalation, remaining term and owner-retained costs from the lease abstract and operating statements.
  4. Transfer-tax rates confirmed by escrow, not by memory.
  5. A discount rate agreed in advance: either the owner's cost of capital or the Treasury rate plus a stated spread.
  6. An exit cap rate supported by comparables, tested at two percentage points either side.
