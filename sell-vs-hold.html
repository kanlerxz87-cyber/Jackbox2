Jack in the Box · Central Los Angeles W-02 · After-tax sell-versus-hold comparison

Sell before expiry, or hold and renew?

This page turns a sale and a hold into two numbers you can compare after tax. It is one page of arithmetic over inputs you supply from verified documents. It runs entirely in your browser: no network request, no cookie, no local storage, nothing saved. Close the tab and the numbers are gone.

Leave a box blank if you do not have the document. The tool refuses to compute rather than fill a gap with a default. A guessed basis or a guessed cap rate produces a confident wrong answer, which is worse than no answer. Every input below is marked either verified rule (the tax law is fixed and sourced) or your assumption (you must supply it and be able to defend it).

What the comparison does and does not do

It does

  • Compute the federal and California tax on a sale, including §1250 recapture at 25%, NIIT, and the tier the capital gain actually lands in given your other income.
  • Apply the LA City base transfer tax and the Measure ULA surtax, including the cliff at $5.4M and $10.9M.
  • Discount the hold-and-renew rent stream and exit value to today, then subtract stress you choose.
  • Show you which case wins, and whether the answer flips when the stress is switched on.

It does not

  • Tax the hold case's rent. Ordinary income tax on rent depends on your bracket and entity, which are not verified here, so it is excluded rather than guessed. This flatters the hold case.
  • Model depreciation recapture on the renewal capex, or a §1031 exchange. Both are real options and both need your CPA, not a web page.
  • Value the land under a redevelopment scenario. Transit-oriented zoning upside is discussed on Trade Area as context only.
  • Give advice. This is a sensitivity tool. The recommendation is to get the five documents listed on Questions and then run it again.
Reading a result honestly. If the gap between hold and sell is small relative to the size of your assumptions, you do not have an answer, you have noise. Change the discount rate by one point and the cap rate by 50 basis points; if the winner flips, the decision is not yet determined by the data you have.
