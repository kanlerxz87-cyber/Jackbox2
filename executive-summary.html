Executive summary

The bottom line, the three candidate paths with written tests, what is verified, what is missing, and the eight-week sequence that produces a decision.

The decision cannot be made yet, and the honest answer is to say so. Selling before expiry and renewing are both defensible on today's evidence, which is exactly why neither should be chosen. The tenant is contracting but still operates 2,115 restaurants; the county is losing population while metro payroll is growing; the lease and the tax basis — the two inputs that convert an opinion into a number — are private and not yet abstracted. Choosing now would mean guessing.

The sequence that produces a decision

  1. Weeks 1–2 — protect the options. Abstract the lease and reconcile the notice calendar. Nothing else matters if a notice date passes. Gate G1.
  2. Weeks 2–4 — resolve the biggest unknown. Request an estoppel certificate and a written renewal indication, together with store-level financials for the operating entity. Gate G2.
  3. Weeks 2–6 — build the valuation range. Three broker opinions with short-lease comparables, plus a re-leasing and conversion study. Gate G5.
  4. Weeks 2–6 — build the hazard and insurance file. Parcel zone determinations, an engineering opinion, two quotes and a FAIR Plan comparison. Gate G4.
  5. Weeks 4–8 — fix the trade area. Select tracts once, document the rule, pull two ACS vintages for direction rather than a point estimate. Gate G3.
  6. Weeks 4–8 — fix the tax number. Basis, depreciation, entity, residency, transfer taxes, exchange capacity. Gate G6.
  7. Week 8 — compare, stress, decide. One after-tax number against one hold net present value, then base, downside and black swan. Gate G7. Written memo, signed.

Wealth preservation and growth, in plain terms

The owner's exposure today is concentrated in two places that a spreadsheet will not fix: tenant credit and hazard. Tenant credit is public enough to watch — closures, same-store sales, rental revenue, leverage, leadership. Hazard is not: the parcel-level determinations are missing, and an uninsured loss is the one event that can convert a manageable risk into a forced sale. The cheapest risk reduction available now is information, not action: three broker opinions, two insurance quotes, one lease abstract, one engineer's opinion.

On growth, the honest position is that a short-lease drive-through in a county losing population is not a growth asset unless the immediate trade area is growing. That is testable at tract level and is currently untested. Until it is tested, the renewal case rests on the brand, and the brand is shrinking.
