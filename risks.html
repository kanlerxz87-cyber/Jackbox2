Risks for every decision you could make

Your stated objective is to preserve wealth, keep room for growth and hold risk at a manageable level. That means the analysis has to include the outcomes that would make the decision look foolish in hindsight, not only the central case. Every business-as-usual risk and every plausible black swan is listed below against each of the four real options, with the specific test that would detect it and the mitigation that would survive it.

The four options, stated plainly. (1) Sell now, before the term shortens further. (2) Renew or blend-and-extend with the tenant. (3) Hold to expiry and re-tenant or redevelop. (4) Sell into a §1031 exchange, which is a sale with a specific tax treatment rather than a separate option. Option 1 and option 4 share the same market risk; they differ entirely on tax.

1 · Business-as-usual risks, by decision

DecisionPrincipal risksHow you would detect itMitigation that survives stress
Sell now Short-lease discount deepens as term shortens; thin buyer pool for a declining brand; tax leakage from recapture, NIIT and California's ordinary-rate treatment; Measure ULA charged on gross price, not gain; selling into a soft bid because you are the motivated party. Two or three written BOVs at different assumed terms; a CPA after-tax run; a check on whether the price crosses the $5.4M or $10.9M ULA threshold. Run the comparison under stress before listing. If the after-tax number beats the stressed hold case by a margin larger than your pricing error, sell; if it does not, the discount is doing the deciding for you.
Renew or blend-and-extend Tenant same-store sales negative for three years and franchises net-closing; tenant's cost of capital rose sharply; leadership turnover; a renewal at a rent the store cannot cover; remodel or capex obligations falling on you; a long term locking in today's weak rent. Rent-coverage test against store-level sales; the tenant's own disclosed AUV and percentage-rent trend; a broker read on market rent for the box. Negotiate a shorter term with a fixed option rather than a long primary term; require the tenant to fund remodel; keep the rent at or below market so a re-let is possible.
Hold to expiry Vacancy on day one after expiry; re-tenanting cost and downtime; the highest short-lease discount of any option; a building configured for a burger drive-through that few replacement tenants want. The lease's notice and holdover provisions; a broker view on re-let demand and conversion cost; the specialisation of the improvements. Start marketing twelve to eighteen months before expiry, not after; budget a tenant-improvement reserve; confirm the zoning allows alternative uses.
Sell into a §1031 exchange All the market and tax-filing risks of a sale, plus the 45-day identification and 180-day closing deadlines; a replacement property that is worse than what you sold; failure to identify in time converts the exchange into a taxable sale. Counsel review of the exchange calendar before listing; identification of two or three replacement candidates before the sale closes. Do not list without a qualified intermediary engaged and a written calendar. The deadlines are statutory and are not extendable for market conditions.

2 · Black-swan risks and how they hit all four options at once

EventVerified basisWhat it does to the assetThe test that would have caught it
Major earthquake USGS: 60% chance of ≥M6.7 in the LA region within 30 years; 46% at ≥M7.0. MF-1964 characterises the region as facing "the greatest seismic risk of any part of the United States." Physical damage, rent interruption, a deductible that can exceed a year of rent, and a frozen sale market at the exact moment you would want liquidity. All three at once. Parcel fault and liquefaction zone; design ground shaking; retrofit report; earthquake quote with the deductible stated in dollars. USGS
Wildfire, smoke or evacuation The January 2025 fires destroyed 11,160 housing units in Los Angeles County and contributed to a 64,000-person population loss in the year to January 2026, per the state's own estimate. Direct damage if in a severity zone, smoke and closure losses if not, and a statewide insurance repricing that reaches urban parcels too. Fire hazard severity zone designation; defensible-space obligations; business-interruption cover. CAL FIRE / OSFM
Flood or storm FEMA designates flood hazard zones and the NFIP sets the insurance consequence; a special hazard area makes cover mandatory for a federally backed loan. Ground-floor damage, access loss during the highest-traffic period, lender-mandated insurance, and a reconstruction period during which rent may abate under the lease. FEMA flood zone letter; base flood elevation against slab height; flood quote. FEMA MSC
Tenant insolvency or a large-scale closure programme The tenant closed 86 restaurants in FY2025, 51 through a deliberate block-closure programme, and its total shareholder return from September 2020 to September 2025 was −72% against +114% for the S&P 500. Rejection of the lease in a reorganisation, an abrupt dark store, and a re-let into a soft market with a building designed for one brand. Lease guaranty and its scope; rent-coverage ratio; the tenant's closure list and whether the parcel's trade area appears in it. SEC Form 10-K FY2025
Local economic shock LA metro payroll growth has been 0.5% for three consecutive months to September 2026, with unemployment at 4.4%; county population has fallen for five consecutive years. Quick-service traffic falls before rents do, so the tenant absorbs the shock first and passes it on at the next rent review or renewal. BLS metro payroll and unemployment trend; Census and state population series; tract-level ACS trend once the tract is known. BLS
Insurance market withdrawal The 2025 fire losses drove a statewide repricing and increased reliance on the FAIR Plan as insurer of last resort, regulated by the California Department of Insurance. A premium that resets upward at renewal, a deductible that makes a claim uneconomic, or an outright refusal to quote — any of which can make a property unsaleable to a leveraged buyer. Two independent quotes plus a written renewal indication; a check with the FAIR Plan on availability. CA Dept. of Insurance
Interest-rate shock The 10-year Treasury par yield was 4.85% on 25 November 2026; cap rates for single-tenant retail move with it. Every 100 basis points of cap-rate widening cuts the exit value by roughly a sixth at a 6% cap. That hits the hold case and the sale price simultaneously. Treasury daily par yield curve; a cap-rate sensitivity run in the calculator. U.S. Treasury

4 · What would make the risk unmanageable

Red flags that change the recommendation

  • The parcel sits in a fault-rupture or liquefaction zone and the building is not retrofitted.
  • Insurance is unobtainable or the earthquake deductible exceeds twelve months of rent.
  • The lease has no guaranty and the tenant is a single-asset franchisee.
  • Rent exceeds what a replacement tenant would pay for the box, so a re-let means a permanent rent cut.
  • The renewal requires you to fund a remodel with no rent increase to amortise it.
  • Your after-tax sale proceeds are needed for liquidity within three years.

Green flags that support holding and renewing

  • A corporate guaranty, or a franchisee with multiple units and a clean payment record.
  • Store-level sales cover rent several times over, with margin to absorb a wage increase.
  • The parcel's hazard exposure is low, with insurance available at a stable premium.
  • Zoning permits a higher-value use, giving the land an alternative to the burger tenant.
  • The renewal rent is at or below market, so the asset re-lets easily if the tenant leaves.
  • The current basis is high, so a sale now would realise a small gain.
The asymmetry to keep in view. A sale is reversible in the sense that you keep the proceeds and can buy something else, but it is irreversible in this respect: you permanently give up any upside in the land and any transit-corridor redevelopment option. A renewal is reversible in the sense that you can usually sell a leased asset, but it is irreversible in this respect: you have locked in a rent that a struggling tenant must pay for years. Both are real commitments. That is why neither should be made before the gates on Decision Gates clear.
