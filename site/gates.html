Zero of twelve decision-critical gates are closed. That is the honest headline of this entire project. Nothing in the economic evidence is strong enough to compensate for not knowing the rent, the term, the entity, the basis or the hazard zone.

How to read a gate. A gate is a question that must be answered with evidence before a specific commitment is made. It is not a task on a project plan. "Blocking" means the owner must not authorise a listing, a letter of intent or a renewal negotiation while that gate is open — because doing so prices the asset from an unknown. "High" means the decision can be framed but not committed. "Medium" means it affects sizing rather than direction.

Sequencing

The order is not arbitrary. Market-testing before the evidence phase closes anchors the owner to a number produced from unknowns — the most expensive mistake available here.

PhaseNameGatesNote
1EvidenceG-01, G-02, G-03, G-04, G-07, G-12All blocking. Nothing else should start. This is roughly two to three weeks of adviser work, not months.
2Market testG-08, G-06, G-05Quotes and a written tenant indication. Doing this before phase 1 risks anchoring the owner to a number produced from unknowns.
3AnalysisG-09, G-10, G-11Model, trade area and zoning headroom. Only now is the sell-versus-hold comparison numerically meaningful.
4Decision—Compare the after-tax sale number against the probability-weighted renewal number, both from verified inputs, and choose against the objective statement.
