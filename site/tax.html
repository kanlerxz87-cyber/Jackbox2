How the layers stack

A worked illustration of the structure. It uses no property-specific numbers, because there are none.

  1. Split the gain. Separate the portion attributable to depreciation (unrecaptured §1250) from the rest. Only the first layer is taxed at 25%; the second follows capital-gain rates.
  2. Federal. 25% maximum on the recapture layer, then 0/15/20% on the remaining long-term gain according to taxable income.
  3. Net investment income tax. 3.8% on the net gain once MAGI exceeds $200,000 single or $250,000 married filing jointly. A triple-net ground lease is normally passive, so the NIIT normally applies — that determination belongs to the adviser.
  4. California. The gain is ordinary income for state purposes. Large gains push into the 11.3% and 12.3% brackets. There is no state capital-gain rate.
  5. Section 1031. Fully deferred, the federal layers on reinvested proceeds collapse toward zero, and the state treatment follows. This is the largest single lever available and it must be arranged before closing.

The cash-flow trap in an installment sale. Under the installment method the capital gain is spread over the payments, but depreciation-recapture ordinary income must be recognised in the year of sale regardless. An owner who sells on seller financing can therefore owe tax on money not yet received.
