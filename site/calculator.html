Sell-versus-hold worksheet

W-02 and R-19: the private worksheet extended to a full after-tax sale comparison against a probability-weighted renewal case. Nothing is transmitted, nothing is stored, and no value you type leaves this tab.

🔒
This page has no network capability. There is no form action, no fetch, no XMLHttpRequest, no local storage, no cookie, and no third-party script. Every calculation happens in the JavaScript on this page, in your browser. Close the tab and everything you typed is gone. It ships with placeholder values on purpose — do not type a real address or parcel number into it; you do not need to.

Read this before trusting a number. This worksheet is arithmetic, not analysis. It will happily compute a confident answer from invented inputs. Its only defence is that every input is visible, labelled as verified or assumed, and pre-filled with a placeholder of 1 so that an untouched page produces an obviously meaningless result rather than a plausible-looking one. Real figures for the tax rates are verified and linked on the after-tax page; the property figures are not known yet.

Why this is deliberately unhelpful right now

The page opens with every property input set to 1. That produces a meaningless comparison on purpose. A calculator that pre-filled a "typical" rent or a "market" cap rate would be doing the one thing this project refuses to do — inventing a number and then treating it as data.

What this worksheet does well is show you the shape of the decision: how much the after-tax number moves when the §1031 share changes, and how quickly the hold value collapses when the renewal probability falls. Those relationships are true regardless of the missing inputs, and they are the part an adviser should be thinking about while the gate items are collected.

Formulas used

  • Net sale price = price × (1 − selling costs)
  • Recapture gain = min(accumulated depreciation, total gain)
  • Remaining gain = total gain − recapture gain
  • Federal = recapture × 25% + remaining × CG rate
  • NIIT = (recapture + remaining) × 3.8%, if applicable
  • California = total gain × state rate
  • Deferred share reduces each of the above proportionally, per §1031
  • Hold = Σ rent×(1+g)^t ÷ (1+d)^t for t = 1…term, plus reversion ÷ (1+d)^term
  • Weighted hold = hold × renewal probability

This is a simplification of the tax code, not an implementation of it. It ignores phase-outs, the interaction of the NIIT threshold with other income, state adjustments, and the taxpayer's actual bracket. Use it to see the shape of the trade-off, then hand the inputs to an adviser.
