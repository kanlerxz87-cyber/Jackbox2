W-02: the private worksheet extended to a full sell-versus-hold comparison, with no winner chosen until verified inputs exist.

Nothing on this page is a valuation. There is no verified rent, no verified basis, no verified term and no verified comparable. What follows is (a) verified statements of tax law, and (b) worked formula templates with the inputs left as placeholders. W-02 and R-04 require the comparison to be built without selecting a winner until verified inputs exist — which is exactly what this is.

The one rule this page follows. Every line of the sale template has an empty value column. Not zero, not an estimate, not a range. Empty. A reader can see precisely which numbers are known (the tax rates) and which are not (everything else).

The stacking that surprises owners. Gains on this asset can be taxed in three layers at once: up to 25% federal on the unrecaptured section 1250 portion, up to 20% federal on the rest, 3.8% net investment income tax on top, and then California at up to 12.3% with no capital-gain preference. The combined marginal rate on the recapture layer can exceed 40%. This is why the section 1031 question is not a detail — it is often the difference between two very different outcomes for the same sale price.

Hold case — why it is not the "safe" option

The residual path is also a bet, and it is worth naming the three things it requires.

  • Hold is not the residual option. It is a bet that (a) the tenant renews, (b) the rent resets upward at a real rate, and (c) the land's alternative use value rises.
  • Verified against evidence: (a) is doubtful on the tenant's own disclosures, (b) is constrained by whatever the option mechanism says, and (c) requires zoning headroom (TA-18) plus construction activity, which is flat at +0.2% 12-month change (IM-08).
  • Hold also carries the non-financial costs: another 5 to 9 years of single-tenant concentration, another cycle of California insurance availability risk, and the illiquidity of a small commercial asset.

Short-lease discount — unverified, and here is how to fix it

The most powerful valuation input for this asset, and the one no official dataset publishes.

What it should measure. The percentage spread a buyer demands on a ground lease with under 10 years of term versus the same lease with 20+ years. It is the single most powerful valuation input for this asset and it is not observable in any official dataset.

What happened when we looked. Commercial data providers (CoStar, Reonomy, Crexi, RCA) hold the quote data but are paywalled and are not official government sources, so they cannot satisfy the verification rule.

Status. unverified — flagged as I-04. No value is published.

Three routes to a real number

  1. Request written bid indications from three net-lease buyers on this specific asset, not verbal guidance.
  2. Ask two brokers with QSR net-lease books for anonymised spread data behind a confidentiality undertaking, then have the owner's adviser confirm it against closed sales in the same county.
  3. Check the county recorder for recorded transfers of comparable Jack in the Box parcels and pull the documentary transfer tax, which reveals the recorded price. That is an official route to a real comp.

The free route. Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk grant deed index plus the documentary transfer tax stamp. This is public record and is the only way to build a verified comp set without buying data.
