Risk register

Risks for every decision path, including the correlated black-swan events that could take rent, repairs and exit liquidity at the same time.

No probability is assigned to any event on this page. Assigning one without an insured loss model would be fabrication. The comparisons are ordinal — which risk is worse than which — not cardinal. Where verified evidence exists it is quoted with its source; where the point is structural reasoning, it is labelled as an assumption.

The one risk that is not in the property at all. Concentration. If this parcel is a large share of the owner's net worth, then holding it is a decision about the owner's balance sheet, not about the building — and it may justify a sale regardless of price. That is why gate G-12 exists: the objective function must be stated before the analysis can conclude anything.

The design principle. Do not hold a single, illiquid, uninsured-tail asset in a seismic region for a horizon longer than the lease term that justifies it. That is the risk-management conclusion, and it is independent of the price.

Wealth preservation versus growth

The brief asks for a balance that preserves wealth while keeping growth available at a manageable risk level. Those two goals point in opposite directions here, and pretending otherwise would be the least useful thing this site could do.

What holding preserves

  • Optionality. The owner keeps the reversion and any land-value upside.
  • Tax deferral by simply not selling.
  • Income, if and while the tenant pays.

What holding costs

  • Unresolved concentration in one tenant, one building, one seismic region.
  • Exposure to an uncapped regulatory cost mechanism (AB 1228) that drives the tenant's closures.
  • Illiquidity at exactly the moment a correlated shock would make it most costly.
  • Term decay: every year of expiry approaching widens the buyer's spread, so waiting has a price even if nothing goes wrong.

The balance point. A manageable risk level here is not achieved by choosing hold or sell in the abstract. It is achieved by (1) closing the blocking gates, (2) testing the market and the tenant at the same time, and (3) sizing the decision against a written objective statement — so that whichever path is chosen, the concentration and the tail are deliberate rather than inherited.
