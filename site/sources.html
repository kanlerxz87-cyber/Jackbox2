How the tiering works. Tier 1 is an official primary document — a filing, a statistical release, a statute, a regulator's page. Only Tier 1 may be the basis of a published value. Tier 2 is an official agency series republished by another official body (FRED republishing Freddie Mac and the Federal Reserve); acceptable because the underlying publisher is named. Tier 3 is any non-official source; it is used only to locate a Tier 1 document and is never a basis for a value. Commercial data providers are Tier 3 by definition, which is why the comparable-sales and short-lease-discount questions are flagged rather than answered.

Environment limitation, disclosed. This session's research channel could read web pages but could not download binary files or call data APIs. Two consequences are visible in the ledger: the California Department of Finance projection workbooks (S-09) could not be opened, so no population projection is published; and the Census data API refused queries without a key, so no tract-level ACS pull was possible. Both are recorded as gaps, not worked around with estimates.
