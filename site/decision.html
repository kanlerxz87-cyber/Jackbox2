What the verified evidence says

  • Regional indicators are mixed context, not a trade-area forecast. Metro employment, city population and sector measures have different geographies and periods; none is a substitute for tract-level demand, traffic, competition or parcel economics.
  • Tenant-wide operating results are not this store's results. Company disclosures report system-level sales and unit counts; they do not establish the specific lessee, guarantor, store sales, rent coverage, or renewal intent for this property.
  • Tax rules do not select the outcome. A potential §1031 exchange has eligibility, timing, reinvestment and tax-basis conditions. A CPA must compare the owner's actual alternatives; the site does not assume deferral or a tax rate.
  • Hazard scenarios are stress tests, not parcel findings or probabilities. Parcel-specific engineering, insurance and loss-of-rent evidence are required before comparing the exposure under sale and hold paths.

What the verified evidence cannot say

  • The rent. Unknown. Neither case can be priced.
  • The term. A five-year range, which cannot be discounted.
  • The obligor. Brand or franchisee — different balance sheets entirely.
  • The basis. Unknown, so the tax layer is unknown.
  • The price. No quote, no comparable, no verified spread.
  • The hazards. No parcel-level zone status for seismic, flood or fire.

Evidence that would support evaluating a hold case

  • A firm term extending meaningfully past 2032, with no tenant termination right.
  • A corporate or well-capitalised guaranty behind the rent.
  • A renewal option with a fair-market-value reset rather than a fixed bump.
  • Parcel-level hazard findings that are benign, plus affordable insurance.
  • Zoning headroom that gives the land a credible alternative use.
  • Three written quotes that come in materially above the owner's hold-case present value.

These are evidence items to test—not conclusions, weights, or a preference for holding. The corresponding sale case must be tested against the same verified inputs.

Three paths deserve particular attention.

  • D-5, sale-leaseback to the tenant. Almost nobody prices this. A tenant that owns its own rent can rationally pay more than any investor, because it is buying the elimination of its own lease obligation. The verified filing shows the tenant already acquires assets for sale-and-leaseback and has named "selling real estate" in its plan, which makes it a possible counterparty whose interest and ability remain unverified.
  • D-6, 1031 exchange. The largest single lever on the after-tax outcome and the easiest to botch. It must be prepared before listing, not after the closing.
  • D-7, defer action. The default while evidence is gathered. It preserves optionality but leaves the owner exposed to lease, tenant, market, hazard and liquidity risks.

Why public economic activity cannot decide this case alone

The requested local indicators are inputs to diligence, not a direct valuation of the property or a prediction of renewal.

  1. Tenant-wide sales, closures and leverage do not identify the actual tenant, guarantor, store cash flow, or rent coverage for this lease.
  2. Metro, city, county and tract measures describe different geographies. None should be presented as the property's trade area until secure parcel-based tract selection and a defensible drive-time/catchment method are completed.
  3. School, income, population, jobs, access, competition and land-use evidence can inform demand, but need consistent vintages, local geographies and source-specific limitations.
  4. Sale value depends on verified buyer bids, remaining term, rent, options, credit, title, condition and market conditions. Hold value depends on renewal terms, cash flows, costs and risk.
  5. Tax consequences and the owner's goals (income, liquidity, diversification, legacy and risk tolerance) can materially change the preferred path.

Decision rule: do not rank sale, renewal or deferral until the same verified assumptions are applied to both after-tax cases, uncertainties are shown, and the owner/advisers confirm the objectives and risk limits.
