Decision gates

Seven gates stand between today and a decision you can defend. Each has a defined pass criterion: what "done" means, who signs it off, and what document proves it. A gate is only green when that document exists. Nothing on this board requires you to publish anything — the proof stays private, and only the fact that it exists gets recorded here.

What "green" means, and what it does not

Green means

  • A named professional has put the answer in writing.
  • The document exists in your private workspace and can be shown to a lender, buyer or court if needed.
  • The figure on the site, if any, traces to an official source or is left blank.

Green does not mean

  • That the answer is favourable. A green gate can kill the renewal case entirely, which is a useful outcome.
  • That you must publish the document. Only the fact that it exists is recorded.
  • That the decision is made. Green gates make the comparison valid; they do not make the choice for you.

The rule this board exists to enforce. A renewal signed, or a sale agreed, before G1, G4 and G5 are green is a decision made without knowing the expiry date, without knowing the after-tax proceeds, and without knowing what the market pays for the remaining term. Each of those can individually reverse the answer. Signing first and modelling later is how a good asset gets sold badly.
If you are time-constrained. The one date that matters is the earliest notice deadline in the lease. Until G1 tells you what that date is, treat the whole 2030–2035 window as live and assume you may need to act a full 12 months before the earliest possible expiry. That gives you until roughly 2029 — comfortable, but not indefinite, and the broker and CPA work should be finished well before the notice window opens, not during it.
